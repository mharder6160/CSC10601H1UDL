Assessment and Feedback Guidance

UDL-aligned programming assignment rubric

Criteria Weight What to look for
Functional correctness 40% Program meets requirements and produces expected outputs.
Code quality and readability 20% Naming, structure, indentation, and understandable logic.
Testing and debugging evidence 20% Student demonstrates a testing process and interprets results.
Explanation and reflection 20% Student explains choices, limitations, or lessons learned.

Performance level descriptors

  • Exceeds: Complete, correct, and well-explained with robust testing.
  • Meets: Core requirements complete and mostly correct with adequate testing.
  • Developing: Partial completion or recurring correctness issues.
  • Needs revision: Major requirements missing or non-runnable submission.

Academic integrity and collaboration guidance

Integrity baseline

  • Students submit their own code unless collaboration is explicitly allowed.
  • Reusing code from peers, the internet, or AI tools without permission and attribution is not allowed.
  • Institutional policy and course-specific AI expectations both apply.

Collaboration guidance language

  • Allowed: discussing concepts, pseudocode, and debugging strategies.
  • Not allowed: sharing full solutions, copying runnable code, or submitting another person's work.
  • If pair or group activities occur, require an individual accountability artifact.
